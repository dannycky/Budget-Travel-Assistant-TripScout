## Commute Tips
You can see much of the city on foot.
- By public transport

Public transport in Dublin consists of trains, trams and buses. Unlike many other European capitals, the rail network in Dublin is quite limited, so buses are by far the main mode of public transport. Public transport is not run by a single agency, but by a number of state-contracted operators, and most information is provided separately on each operator's website. Trains are run by [Irish Rail](https://www.irishrail.ie/) and trams by [Luas](https://luas.ie/), while most buses are run by [Dublin Bus](https://www.dublinbus.ie/), except some local buses in suburban areas which are run by [Go-Ahead Ireland](https://www.goaheadireland.ie/).

[Transport for Ireland (TFI)](https://www.transportforireland.ie/) is the umbrella brand for public transport in Ireland, although its website primarily just directs you to the individual operators websites for information. However, the [TFI Journey Planner](https://www.transportforireland.ie/plan-a-journey/) is a good way to plan your journey across different modes, and the [Live Departures](https://www.transportforireland.ie/live-travel-info-service-updates/live-departures/) provides real time information for all rail and bus stops. TFI also provides a number of [smartphone apps](https://www.transportforireland.ie/available-apps/) including a journey planner and real time departures. The journey planner app also provides real time departures, so you don't need to download both. The TFI Journey Planner and Live Departures are also integrated into Google Transit and are available within Google Maps.
- Tickets and Leap Card

You can use the Leap Card (see below) to pay on all forms of public transport in Dublin and most of Ireland. There is a flat fare in place for all journeys on Dublin Bus and Luas when paying with a Leap Card: €2 for adults and €0.65 for children (Aug 2026). Children under 5 travel for free. You cannot pay using contactless bank cards though this is planned to be rolled out in the future.

## Budget Drinks
- **Peadar Kearney's** — Named for Peadar Ó Cearnaígh (1883-1942) who penned Amhráin na bhFiann, Ireland's national anthem, and was uncle to the three Behans, authors and prolific drinkers all. The pub is a great spot for pre- and post-gig drinks next to Olympia Theatre, with a young crowd and live music from up and coming  [Website](http://www.peadarkearneys.ie/)
- **The Cobblestone** — They describe themselves as "a drinking pub with a music problem" and have nightly trad sessions. [Website](http://cobblestonepub.ie/)
- **Frank Ryans** — This pub and pizzeria has friendly bar staff and a mixed crowd of students, law types, trendies and locals. Expect weekly trad nights interspersed with Rockabilly, Country and Soul on the jukebox. [Website](https://www.frankryanspizza.ie/)
- **O'Donoghue's** — With two bars across a courtyard, it's famous for impromptu live music, and where folk group The Dubliners were formed. They have rooms. [Website](https://www.odonoghues.ie/)
- **The Barge** — Friendly trad pub with four areas, good food and great decor. [Website](https://www.mccaffertysatthebarge.com/)
- **Hartigan's** — Popular student bar, good choice after international rugby matches.
- **The Brazen Head** — Surely the oldest pub in Dublin, founded in 1198 - Ireland's oldest, in Athlone, is from 900 AD. Wonderful on warm, dry summer nights. Live trad music and friendly atmosphere. One of the bars is covered in signed currency notes, there's quite a collection but lacking any bitcoins. [Website](https://www.brazenhead.com/)
- **Ned O'Shea's** — Decent food and live music and dancing. Ned O’Shea (1937-2018) was a famous trad musician from Kerry. [Website](https://www.nedosheas.ie/)
- **John Fallon's** — Small friendly local pub. [Website](https://johnfallon.ie/)
- **The Oval** — Good for drink and food, does an excellent Irish stew. Attracts a mixed age group. Lots of pictures of old Irish celebrities with a tribute to the Quiet Man. [Website](http://theovalbar.com/)
