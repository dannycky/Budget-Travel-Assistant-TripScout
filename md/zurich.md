- Public transport

Zurich is famous for its highly efficient, clean and safe public transport system, managed by the [Zürcher Verkehrsverbund](http://www.zvv.ch/zvv/en/home.html) (ZVV) which covers the entire canton of Zurich and extends into nearby regions. The network includes trams, buses, S-Bahn (suburban trains), cable cars, and boats. The size and complexity of the network may be daunting at first, but you will soon realize that there are many ways to get from one place to another, all efficient.

Tickets and timetable information for Switzerland is available on [sbb.ch](https://www.sbb.ch/en) or can be obtained using the [SBB Mobile](https://www.sbb.ch/en/timetable/mobile-apps/sbb-mobile.html) ([iOS](https://apps.apple.com/app/id294855237), [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ch.sbb.mobile.android.b2c) apps) (requires a working internet connection). Alternatively, use [ZVV's own timetable app](https://www.zvv.ch/zvv/de/service/apps/zvv-app.html) or purchase tickets with the [ZVV-Tickets app](https://www.zvv.ch/zvv/de/service/apps/zvv-app.html). The free Wemlin App ([iOS](https://apps.apple.com/app/id293648832), [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.wemlin.android)) gives you offline access to timetable information and network maps for the canton of Zurich area without internet connection and is therefore ideally for on the go usage in case you don't want to use data roaming. 

You may want to consider using the EasyRide function of the SBB Mobile app, which allows you to "check-in" to rides and calculates the cheapest ticket charge at the end of the day.

The Zurich transit system is divided into numerous fare zones. A ticket for zone 110, which covers the city proper, will suffice for most travelers. The more zones you pass through, the more you'll have to pay for your journey. Each zone has the same price, except Zurich (110) and Winterthur (120), which cost double. There are single tickets, punch cards (Mehrfahrtkarten, these provide six rides for the price of five), day cards (these are designed for round trips and valid for 24 hours), monthly cards, and annual cards. - **Grossmünster** (Tower Fr.&nbsp;4, Fr.&nbsp;2 for students) — Old Romanesque church, symbol of reformed Zurich, where reformer Huldrych Zwingli was appointed the people's priest in 1519. Go up the tower for a great view of Zurich, though the stairs can be quite small and steep. [Website](https://www.grossmuenster.ch)
- **Fraumünster** (Fr. 5) — Old Gothic church (former convent) with window paintings made by Marc Chagall. No photos or videos allowed inside. [Website](http://www.fraumuenster.ch/en)
- **Landesmuseum** (Fr. 13/10 (free with Zürich Card)) — This is the place to go if you want to learn more about the history of Switzerland. There are permanent exhibitions on history, archaeology and art. Changing temporary exhibitions usually revolve around Swiss topics. [Website](https://www.landesmuseum.ch/en)
- **Sechseläuten (Sächsilüüte)** (Free) — On the third Monday of April, the guilds of Zurich celebrate their traditional spring festival with the burning of the snow man (Böögg). A procession of several hundreds of people with historical guild costumes and horses takes place in the centre of the town.

### Culture
- **Langstrasse** — Red light district of Zurich, with more drug dealers and police than usual, but interesting because even this most notorious spot in Switzerland is so clean and safe. The area is the most overtly multicultural spot of the town. Ateliers and stylish bars sit side by side the strip clubs.
- **Niederdorf** — The old town offers beautiful alleys, restaurants and shopping mainly aimed at younger consumers. In the evenings, people visit the Niederdorf's many bars.
